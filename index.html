Our mission

Every disease starts somewhere in a tissue. To see what goes wrong, we first need a clear, shared picture of what healthy tissue looks like, cell by cell, and we need it to be free for everyone.

SAHA builds that picture. We measure thousands of genes and dozens of proteins in place, on the same tissue section a pathologist reads, and release the results openly so any lab, clinic or student can use them.

About SAHA

SAHA maps how genes, proteins, cells and tissues are organized and interact across the human body, using spatial omics data from donors of varied ages, sexes and ancestries. The goal is a shared reference that improves our understanding of human biology, diagnostics and precision medicine.

Organs

Appendix, bone marrow, colon, ileum, liver, lymph node, pancreas, prostate, rectum and stomach, plus circulating PBMCs. More organs are planned.

Health and disease

Healthy reference tissue, with inflammatory bowel disease, colorectal, pancreatic and prostate cancer as comparators.

Platforms

CosMx RNA (1K–19K-plex) and 64-plex protein, Xenium (377-plex to 5K), with matched H&E, linked to histopathology annotations and clinical metadata.

Open by design

FAIR data in SpatialData format under CC-BY 4.0, analysis code under MIT, and an interactive browser.

From tissue to open atlas

  1. Donated tissueHealthy and diseased tissue, given by patients and donors, selected and annotated by pathologists.
  2. Measured in placeRNA, protein and H&E captured on the same tissue, so every cell keeps its neighbourhood.
  3. IntegratedCells segmented, typed and matched across platforms and organs into one reference.
  4. Shared openlyData, code and browsers released free under open licences for anyone to reuse.

Why it matters

A baseline for medicine

Knowing what normal looks like at single-cell resolution helps pathologists and researchers spot the earliest changes in disease.

Diseases side by side

Inflammatory bowel disease and colorectal, pancreatic and prostate cancer profiled with the same methods as healthy tissue, so differences are real, not technical.

Fuel for AI

Matched histology and molecular data teach models to read molecular states from routine H&E slides that every hospital already makes.

Open to all

No paywalls. Students, small labs and hospitals anywhere get the same data as the largest centres.

Frequently asked questions

Is SAHA data free to use?

Yes. Data are released under CC-BY 4.0 and code under MIT: reuse them for any purpose, with attribution.

When will the data be publicly downloadable?

Release v1 is shared with reviewers now. Public download through the AWS Open Data Program and Zenodo opens when the atlas paper is published. Until then, request access.

Can my institution contribute data?

Yes. We welcome spatial omics data on human tissue. See the submission guide or write to us.

How is donor privacy protected?

Released data are de-identified. Data with additional consent restrictions, such as the astronaut blood samples, are shared only under controlled access.
